# 12 Principles of Animation — Motion Design

Motion design is where the 12 principles are most visible. This guide maps
each principle to brand motion, kinetic typography, and logo animation.

## 1. Squash and Stretch
- Pressure and release: a logo stamp squashes on impact and stretches as it
  resets.
- Keep volume constant (equal scaling on both axes).

## 2. Anticipation
- A motion piece opens with a pose hold; kinetic type leans before it types.

## 3. Staging
- The motion logo is legible within the first second; the hero text is
  clear before any complex animation begins.

## 4. Straight Ahead / Pose to Pose
- **Pose to Pose:** define keyframes and easing, then fill in.
- **Straight Ahead:** a continuous reveal, like a line drawing itself.

## 5. Follow Through and Overlapping
- A wipe reveal: elements at the edges lag behind the center.
- A brand's movement "follow-through" after the main logo settles.

## 6. Slow In and Slow Out
- Easing curves control the motion; strong `ease-out` for "settling."

## 7. Arcs
- Natural motion follows arcs; mechanical motion (spinning wheels, car
  hoods) is near-straight.

## 8. Secondary Action
- A brand's typography may be accompanied by a subtle secondary element
  that reinforces the motion (a color wash, a particle field).

## 9. Timing
- The number of frames per unit of time = speed. Match the motion to the
  audio, music, and speech.

## 10. Exaggeration
- Motion design exaggerates more than UI motion; the hero move is the most
  expressive.

## 11. Solid Drawing
- Anchor points, vector paths, art direction, and — even in 3D — the
  silhouette as the "line."

## 12. Appeal
- The brand's motion must be memorable and coherent; a motion tag that
  "feels on-brand."

## Workflow
1. Plan the story (what to show, and in which order)
2. Block the poses (pose-to-pose)
3. Add timing, easing, and arcs
4. Refine with secondary action and exaggeration
5. Review on real devices

## Quick checklist
- [ ] One clear focal point within the first second
- [ ] Timing matches the audio/music
- [ ] Easing and arcs chosen deliberately
- [ ] Secondary motion reinforces the main action
- [ ] Motion feels on-brand (appeal)
