# 12 Principles of Animation — Game Development

## 1. Squash and Stretch
- Character landing: the feet squash, the body stretches.
- Cups, barrels, and dropped items deform on impact and spring back.

## 2. Anticipation
- A swing, a jump, a melee strike: every attack is preceded by a wind-up.

## 3. Staging
- The camera and lighting guide the player's eye to the actionable target.
- A health-bar pulse, a damage flash: staged to lead the story of the
  fight.

## 4. Straight Ahead / Pose to Pose
- Animator poses the key frames (pose-to-pose), then the engine fills the
  "straight-ahead" movement in between.

## 5. Follow Through and Overlapping
- Hair, capes, and loose gear lag behind the body.
- A hitbox: the character keeps drifting after receiving damage.

## 6. Slow In and Slow Out
- Acceleration and deceleration of movement; heavy enemies move slowly,
  light enemies quickly.

## 7. Arcs
- Character movement follows arcs; a sword swing, a jump, a dodge.

## 8. Secondary Action
- A character walking while adjusting a weapon or looking at an object.

## 9. Timing
- Frame counts per in-game action define character weight and personality.
- A quick dodge has fewer frames than a heavy armored walk.

## 10. Exaggeration
- A stunned character may have exaggerated physics; a fast-fist-hit zooms.

## 11. Solid Drawing
- 3D characters need proper anatomy, weight, and silhouette — even with
  rigging and animation tools.

## 12. Appeal
- Characters that are memorable, cute or menacing, but always
  "interesting" and readable at a distance.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Every attack has a wind-up (anticipation)
- [ ] Landing will squash (squash & stretch)
- [ ] Movement follows arcs (arcos)
- [ ] Heavy and light are timed differently (timing)
- [ ] Hair/gear follow with lag (follow through)
- [ ] Characters are readable and memorable (appeal)
