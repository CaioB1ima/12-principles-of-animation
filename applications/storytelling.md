# 12 Principles of Animation — Storytelling

Animation is the language of narrative. The 12 principles shape how a
story moves — not just how a character looks, but how it feels.

## 1. Squash and Stretch
- A fall, a collision, a surprise: weight changes communicate emotion.

## 2. Anticipation
- Tension is built with a preparatory pose: a gun is raised before the
  trigger is pulled; a character leans forward before speaking.

## 3. Staging
- The camera and lighting place the audience exactly where the emotion is;
  no detail competes with the story.

## 4. Straight Ahead / Pose to Pose
- Pose to pose for scripted scenes with clear dramatic beats.
- Straight ahead for dream sequences, internal monologue, and surreal
  movement.

## 5. Follow Through and Overlapping
- A character who stops still has hair and clothes that keep moving.

## 6. Slow In and Slow Out
- The emotional arc: moments of weight and pause, moments of release.

## 7. Arcs
- Character movement follows arcs; even dialogue blocks are staged along
  arcs.

## 8. Secondary Action
- Speech: a hand, a glance, a shift of weight — the secondary action
  carries emotion.

## 9. Timing
- Emotional timing is the most important part: a pause can carry more than
  a line.

## 10. Exaggeration
- Emotional expression is exaggerated to the point of clarity, not realism.

## 11. Solid Drawing
- Character design, silhouette, and composition must read without
  dialogue.

## 12. Appeal
- The audience must care — the character is real, interesting, and visually
  engaging.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Anticipation builds tension before the key line/action
- [ ] Pauses are used with intention (timing)
- [ ] Secondary action carries emotion (expression + action)
- [ ] Staging removes any competing focal point
- [ ] Characters are readable without dialogue (appeal)
